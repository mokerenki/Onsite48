# Comparative Research Findings — 2026-08-17

## Verified public product positions

| Platform | Primary job-to-be-done | Public product evidence | Strategic reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Explee | Turn a company website into an always-on outbound pipeline. | Explee says its agents research the market, sharpen ICP, find prospects, write personalized email, handle replies, and book demos. It advertises a B2B company/person search product and a pay-as-you-go motion. [1] [2] | A highly legible vertical outcome: more qualified meetings. It compresses the “autonomous agent” story into one buyer-owned KPI. |
| Apollo | Give revenue teams a data-plus-engagement system for prospecting, enrichment, outreach, and pipeline work. | Apollo positions data intelligence and sales engagement together; it claims 40,000 paying customers, 1 million sales professionals, a 240M+ contact database, and multichannel sequences. These are first-party claims. [3] [4] [5] | The incumbent is broad but coherent because data is its wedge and revenue teams are its buyer. It benefits from embedded workflows and switching costs. |
| Manus | Delegate complex online work to a general agent. | Manus documents local-browser operation using existing sessions/active tabs, session authorization, dedicated task tabs, action logs, and a local-versus-cloud-browser distinction. [6] [7] | It sells a visceral “watch the agent do the work” experience. Its value proposition is broad, but the interaction is simple: assign a goal and observe progress. |
| Onsite48 | Provide secure, always-on agents that proactively operate business workflows. | Synth’s public page promises morning briefings, six functional business wings, 200+ connectors, autonomous business workflows, and entry pricing. [8] | The aspirational position is compelling, but it spans several difficult categories before one proof point is established. The implementation should narrow to an owned wedge first. |

## Manus attention mechanics — evidence versus inference

IBM reported that Manus attracted public attention within a week of launch; the startup claimed 2 million people on a waitlist while access was invitation-only. IBM credited a combination of visible multi-tool task execution and progressive feedback in the experience, but also cautioned that an impressive demo differs from enterprise integration. [9] SCMP reported that the invite-only approach ended after a funding event and rising competition. [10]

**Evidence-based lesson:** scarcity amplified attention, but it amplified an already legible demo. The durable artifact was not “a waitlist”; it was a visible, replayable experience in which a system produced work across research, browsing, and tools. Artificially reproducing invite scarcity without a reliable proof loop would damage trust.

## Design implications for Onsite48

1. Do not compete with Apollo on contact database depth or sales-suite breadth, or with Explee on autonomous cold-email infrastructure.
2. Do not launch all six business wings as independently complete products. The current landing-page promise exceeds current verified integration/reliability maturity.
3. Make **Marketing Operations** the paid wedge: a user connects selected sources, Synth produces a daily evidence-backed action board, drafts work, and completes only approved downstream actions.
4. Treat local-browser operation as a trust/coverage feature for authenticated tools—not as the only public headline. It must first be reliable, observable, and approval-safe.
5. Put proof before hype: publish time-boxed, repeatable before/after workflow demonstrations with real source evidence, clear human approval points, and measurable outcome metrics.
